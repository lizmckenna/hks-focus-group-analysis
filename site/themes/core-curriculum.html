Core Curriculum: Structure, Flexibility, and Program Identity

47 utterances across 5 programs

"It's hard to have intentionality when you don't have any core. Most people actually don't know where they're going and they end up with a hodgepodge of classes that don't really align in any way." MIXED participant · MPA dual degree student on having too little structure

Analysis

Core curriculum is the theme that most clearly splits along degree lines. MPP students with a large required core want more flexibility and leveling. MPA dual-degree students with almost no core want more structure and coherence. MPAID students are broadly satisfied but want their core updated to reflect the evolving field. The shared request across all groups: let the core build toward something intentional, not just check boxes. Notably, MPP1s (currently navigating core requirements) express more frustration than MPP2s (who have more distance from the experience), suggesting that sentiment about the core may shift over the course of the degree.

What's Working

The MPAID core is praised for coherence, with courses that connect to each other and build sequentially. The MPP core's negotiations course and some economics tracks are valued. Joint-degree students who have a structured pathway (like MPP/HBS) describe their degree as intentional and well-directed.

Areas for Improvement

MPP1s want core requirements to accommodate different preparation levels. Advanced students get stuck in introductory courses they have already mastered. MPA dual-degree students describe a 'hodgepodge' of classes with no coherent throughline. Multiple students want the core redesigned around professional functions and skills rather than academic topics. MPAID students want more U.S. development content alongside international.

In Their Words

Curated quotes that illustrate the range of perspectives on this theme.

"It's hard to have intentionality when you don't have any core. Most people actually don't know where they're going and they end up with a hodgepodge of classes that don't really align in any way." MIXED · MPA dual degree student on having too little structure
"We have too much core. I think that my degree feels very intentional. I know exactly what I'm doing." MIXED · MPP/HBS joint student, by contrast, has clear direction because of structured requirements
"When people say get rid of core classes, what they really mean is adjust for different learning levels. A degree has to have core requirements, but more optionality within core, adjusted to learning levels, is key." MPP1 · Not anti-core, but pro-leveling
"We need to stop thinking about topics and start thinking about functions. If we were to have an end-to-end workflow of something that a government does, what are the skills that I need? There needs to be a finance and budgeting class. There should be an operations class. There should be a leadership class." MIXED · Skills-based core redesign: build around professional functions, not academic departments
"The MPA/ID needs to be rethought. Some of these classes have not been restructured in 25 years." MIXED · Even the praised program needs updating
"If people who are more advanced can opt into more difficult courses, then the Core can adjust to meet the needs of students who are struggling. One of the challenges is people's questions aren't being answered enough in stats or econ." MPP1 · Leveling as a solution to mixed preparation
Discussion question for the task force

HKS already has some pilot efforts around tracks and demonstrated competency. Should these be expanded, deepened, or restructured? Specifically, should the core be reorganized around professional functions and skills (e.g., budgeting, operations, strategy) rather than academic disciplines?
