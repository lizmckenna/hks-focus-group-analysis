Feedback & Assessment: Timing, Quality, and Accountability

34 utterances across 5 programs

"I find it odd that there aren't more rules or structures around what professors can and can't do around assessment structure, around feedback expectations, timing. I feel like every class you go to, it's kind of a random gamble." MPP2 participant · Desire for structural consistency

Analysis

Assignment feedback emerged as one of the strongest themes across programs. Students describe a system with enormous variance: some professors provide detailed, timely feedback while others return grades weeks late with minimal comments, sometimes delegated entirely to CAs. The frustration is not just about speed. It is about the educational compact. Students who invest heavily in assignments feel shortchanged when feedback does not help them improve. The structural issue is clear: there are few school-wide standards for feedback timing or quality.

What's Working

Several students praised individual professors who provide detailed, thoughtful feedback. One MCMPA student specifically valued knowing a professor (not a TA) read every assignment, even if it took longer. The J-term course 'Being Human' was cited as a model for layered feedback from both professor and TA.

Areas for Improvement

Feedback timing is unpredictable. One student reported not receiving a single grade back the entire semester in two classes. Course evaluations are due before grades are returned, undermining the feedback loop. Students want clearer expectations about what feedback to expect and when, even if it varies by class.

In Their Words

Curated quotes that illustrate the range of perspectives on this theme.

"I find it odd that there aren't more rules or structures around what professors can and can't do around assessment structure, around feedback expectations, timing. I feel like every class you go to, it's kind of a random gamble." MPP2 · Desire for structural consistency
"I had two classes last semester that I didn't get a single grade back the entire semester. I got A's in both the classes." MCMPA · Grades without feedback defeats the purpose
"I've had a very different experience. Not the timeline, but knowing that the instructor or professor will be reading every assignment and not a TA. I'm willing to wait that period so I get that full feedback. Very detailed feedback." MCMPA · Quality can compensate for speed
"We're expected to submit reviews of the professors before we even get our grades back at the end of the semester. I think that this is very telling." MCMPA · Broken feedback loop in course evaluations
"One suggestion for the professor would be to just share a detailed rubric and a model answer. That's worked well in the courses that we've had them." MPAID · Concrete solution from students
"There are professors who have gotten feedback returned quickly, but sometimes it's clear that their CAs or TFs are doing the gradings and not them, and that makes a difference." MPP2 · Students notice who actually reads their work
Discussion question for the task force

Should HKS establish minimum standards for feedback timing (e.g., grades returned before course evaluations are due) and require syllabi to specify the feedback structure?
