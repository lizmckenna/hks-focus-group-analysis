Norm Enforcement & the Rules Gap

30 utterances across 5 programs

"If someone walks in late, the teacher should say, this is your warning. The next class, get out. You're late. It's just establishing that if you cross this line, there's going to be a repercussion. The problem is that most lines at the Kennedy School, when you cross them, there's no repercussions." MIXED participant · Joint-degree student comparing to HBS

Analysis

Students across programs identify a persistent gap between stated classroom norms and their enforcement. This tension sits at the intersection of student conduct and faculty accountability. Students recognize that punctuality, device use, and preparation are their responsibilities, but they also see enforcement as a faculty responsibility. When professors don't follow through on stated expectations, the norms lose meaning. The disconnect is sharpest in comparisons with HBS, where joint-degree students describe a culture of consistent consequences that HKS lacks. Students are not asking for draconian rules. They are asking for clarity about what is actually expected and consistency in how those expectations are upheld.

What's Working

Students generally appreciate when professors set clear expectations on day one. Several noted that the best classroom experiences came from professors who were explicit about norms and followed through consistently.

Areas for Improvement

Enforcement is inconsistent across classes. Some professors call out tardiness and phone use; others ignore it entirely. Students describe confusion about what the 'real' rules are versus the stated ones. Joint-degree students with HBS experience describe the contrast as stark.

In Their Words

Curated quotes that illustrate the range of perspectives on this theme.

"If someone walks in late, the teacher should say, this is your warning. The next class, get out. You're late. It's just establishing that if you cross this line, there's going to be a repercussion. The problem is that most lines at the Kennedy School, when you cross them, there's no repercussions." MIXED · Joint-degree student comparing to HBS
"Classes should start on time. Teachers should be ready five to ten minutes before. The norm should be that everyone is on time. The norm is that people aren't on their laptops in the back. I think less than 25% of the people on their computers in a Kennedy School class are actually taking notes." MIXED · On device culture
"I think the spoken rules always sound super reasonable and illegitimate. But behind those external rules, there are also implied rules that everyone understands what they are, but no one mentions them." MCMPA · On the gap between stated and actual norms
"I would definitely be keeping an eye out to see if the professor was treating everyone the same way. If anyone got a pass on this type of behavior, I'd see it much more negatively. Whereas if a professor is treating everyone the exact same way, and expectations were set clearly on the syllabus, I don't think I'd have a problem with it." MPP1 · Fairness and consistency matter more than strictness
"There is a certain merit to holding people to a certain academic standard. I genuinely feel that we should all be collectively holding ourselves to a better preparation for class. On one hand, I don't want other people to be late or underprepared. On the other hand, I don't want people to be intimidated." MPP1 · Tension between standards and psychological safety
"For sure. But if there's this rule that we shouldn't be on technology, is it a rule? It's not a rule. You know what I mean?" MIXED · When unenforced rules aren't really rules
Discussion question for the task force

Should HKS establish school-wide baseline expectations for classroom conduct (punctuality, device use, preparedness) that professors can tighten but not loosen? What would enforcement look like in practice?
